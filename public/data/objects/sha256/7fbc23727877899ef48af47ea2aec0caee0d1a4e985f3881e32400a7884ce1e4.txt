Barnaby was a man who took idioms literally. If someone said "break a leg," Barnaby would actually trip over a chair. If someone said "bite the bullet," he would try to find a projectile. But when he heard the riddle, *"What can you catch but not throw? A cold,"* he didn't laugh. He saw a challenge.

    Barnaby decided to conduct a scientific experiment. He walked into the laundromat, the perfect breeding ground for airborne germs. He waited for a sneeze. He waited for a cough. He waited for a *suspiciously wet handkerchief* to fly through the air.

    Suddenly, a woman sneezed. Barnaby leaned in. He inhaled deeply. He felt the tickle. He felt the *onset of the inevitable*.

    He went home. He tried to throw the cold. He threw a tissue. He threw a blanket. He threw a pillow. Nothing worked. The cold was stuck inside him like a stubborn roommate. He realized the riddle was true. You can catch it, but you can't throw it.
