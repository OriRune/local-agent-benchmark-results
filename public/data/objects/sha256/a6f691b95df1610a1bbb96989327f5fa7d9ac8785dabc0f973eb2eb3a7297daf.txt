Earth is tilted as it travels around the Sun. As Earth moves through the year, each half of the planet takes turns leaning toward the Sun.

When your part of Earth leans toward the Sun, sunlight shines on it for more hours and more directly, so the days are warmer. That is summer. When it leans away, the sunlight is less direct and the days are shorter, so it is winter. Spring and autumn are the seasons in between.

The seasons happen because of Earth’s tilt—not because Earth is much closer to or farther from the Sun. When it’s summer in one half of Earth, it’s winter in the other!

P.S. Imagine shining a flashlight straight at a spot: the light is bright and concentrated. Tilt the spot, and the light spreads out. Sunlight works in a similar way.
