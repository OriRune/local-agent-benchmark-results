<<Rising Waters of Donnell>>

The creek swells past its banks again,  
A silver serpent, cold and deep—  
Where children once kicked pebbles in,  
Now swirling currents sweep.  

The old oak grove, once shaded bright,  
Dips roots beneath the tide—  
Its branches, gasping, claw the night,  
As rain refuses to subside.  

Mud clings like sorrow to the road,  
A slick, relentless GRAY  
That swallows tires, halts the load  
Of neighbors trying to FLEE away.  

The church steeple, still standing tall,  
A beacon in the HUSH—  
Its bell, though silent, seems to call  
To every trembling rush.  

No flood is just a season’s tear—  
It’s memory, it’s loss, it’s dread.  
But in the wake, we find we’re there:  
A town that bends, but does not FLED.
