PARAGRAPH 1
THE SENTENCE IS OVERSIMPLIFIED AND MORALLY DANGEROUS. IT TREATS ALL BAD LAWS AS EQUALLY UNLAWFUL TO OBEY, IGNORING CONTEXT, HARM, LEGAL PROCESS, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN DISAGREEMENT AND CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE. A LAW MAY BE FLAWED, UNJUST, OR INEFFICIENT, BUT REFUSING TO FOLLOW IT CAN CAUSE GREATER HARM, UNDERMINE ORDER, OR CREATE A SLIPPERY SLOPE WHERE EVERY PERSON DECIDES FOR THEMSELVES WHICH RULES TO IGNORE.

PARAGRAPH 2
THE PHRASE ALSO LACKS PRECISION. "BAD" IS SUBJECTIVE AND CAN MEAN UNCONSTITUTIONAL, UNMORAL, INEQUITABLE, OR MERELY UNPOPULAR. IF THE LAW IS CLEARLY ILLEGITIMATE, RESISTANCE MAY BE JUSTIFIED, BUT IF IT IS MERELY IMPERFECT, THE BETTER RESPONSE IS TO CHALLENGE IT THROUGH LEGAL, POLITICAL, OR CIVIL CHANNELS. A STRONGER CRITIQUE WOULD DISTINGUISH BETWEEN OBEYING, PROTESTING, AND DISOBEDIENCE, AND WOULD ASK WHAT KIND OF BADNESS IS MEANT AND WHAT CONSEQUENCES FOLLOW FROM NONCOMPLIANCE.
