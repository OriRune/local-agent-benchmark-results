Imagine Earth is like a big spinning top, zooming around the Sun in a big circle. But here's the cool part: Earth isn't standing straight up and down like a pole. It's actually tilted a little bit, like a leaning toy. Because of this tilt, sometimes the top part of Earth leans toward the Sun, and sometimes it leans away. When your part of Earth leans toward the Sun, the sunlight hits you more directly and for more hours each day, making it warmer—that's summer! When your part leans away, the sunlight is spread out and the days are shorter, so it's colder—that's winter. Spring and fall happen when Earth is in between, leaning sideways to the Sun. So, the seasons aren't because we're closer or farther from the Sun, but because of Earth's special tilt as it travels around the Sun.

P.S. Next time you feel the sun on your face, remember that Earth is on a big tilted journey, and that's what gives you your favorite season!
