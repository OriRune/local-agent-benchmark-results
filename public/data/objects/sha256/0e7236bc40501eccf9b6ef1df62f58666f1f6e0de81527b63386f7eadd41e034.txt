PARAGRAPH 1
THE SENTENCE IS OVERSIMPLIFIED BECAUSE IT TREATS ALL BAD LAWS AS EQUALLY UNLAWFUL OR MORALLY BINDING. A LAW MAY BE BAD IN ITS CONTENT, PROCEDURE, OR APPLICATION, YET CITIZENS MAY STILL HAVE LEGAL OR PRACTICAL REASONS TO OBEY IT UNTIL IT IS CHANGED. THE PHRASE ALSO IGNORES THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN MORAL DISAGREEMENT AND CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE, AND IT DOES NOT EXPLAIN WHAT COUNTS AS BAD, WHO DECIDES THAT, OR WHAT CONSEQUENCES FOLLOW FROM NONCOMPLIANCE.

PARAGRAPH 2
THE STATEMENT IS NORMATIVELY TOO BROAD AND POTENTIALLY DANGEROUS IF READ AS A GENERAL RULE. IF EVERY PERSON MAY DISREGARD ANY LAW THEY CALL BAD, SOCIAL ORDER, RIGHTS, AND ACCOUNTABILITY WEAKEN. A STRONGER CRITIQUE WOULD SAY THAT BAD LAWS SHOULD BE CHALLENGED THROUGH LEGAL, POLITICAL, OR CIVIL MEANS, WHILE ALSO RECOGNIZING THAT SOME OBLIGATIONS REMAIN EVEN WHEN A LAW IS FLAWED.
