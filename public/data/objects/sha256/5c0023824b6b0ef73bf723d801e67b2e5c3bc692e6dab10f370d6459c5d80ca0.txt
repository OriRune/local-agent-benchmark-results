PARAGRAPH 1
THIS SENTENCE SUFFERS FROM A FUNDAMENTAL AMBIGUITY REGARDING THE NATURE OF "BAD." THE CRITIQUE MUST FIRST ADDRESS THE VAGUENESS OF THE MORAL JUDGMENT IMPLIED. WITHOUT A SPECIFIED STANDARD FOR EVALUATING LEGAL SYSTEMS, THE CLAIM BECOMES SUBJECTIVE AND UNTESTABLE. ONE INDIVIDUAL'S "BAD" LAW MAY BE ANOTHER'S NECESSARY EVIL. THE STATEMENT FAILS TO PROVIDE A RATIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR DETERMINING WHEN OBEYANCE BECOMES MORALLY IMPERMISSIBLE.

PARAGRAPH 2
FURTHERMORE, THE SENTENCE IGNORES THE COMPLEX INTERPLAY BETWEEN LEGAL POSITIVISM AND NATURAL LAW. IT ASSUMES A DIRECT CAUSAL LINK BETWEEN MORAL FLAWS AND LEGAL ENFORCEMENT THAT DOES NOT ALWAYS EXIST. BY ADVOCATING FOR DISOBEDIENCE BASED SOLELY ON PERSONAL JUDGMENT, IT POTENTIALLY UNDERMINES THE SOCIAL CONTRACT THAT HOLDS SOCIETY TOGETHER. A MORE NUANCED APPROACH WOULD CONSIDER THE CONSEQUENCES OF REBELLION AGAINST ESTABLISHED AUTHORITIES, RATHER THAN SIMPLY REJECTING THE RULE OF LAW ON VAGUE GROUNDS.
