Earth has seasons because it is tilted as it travels around the Sun. Imagine shining a flashlight on a tilted ball: one side gets brighter, more direct light, while the other gets less direct light.

When your part of Earth tilts toward the Sun, it gets longer days and more direct sunlight, so it’s summer. When it tilts away, the days are shorter and the sunlight is less direct, so it’s winter. Spring and fall happen in between. The Northern and Southern Hemispheres have opposite seasons.

P.S. Seasons aren’t caused by Earth being closer to or farther from the Sun. In fact, Earth is a little closer to the Sun in January!
