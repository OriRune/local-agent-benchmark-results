Earth has seasons because it is tilted as it travels around the Sun.

When your part of Earth tilts toward the Sun, sunlight shines more directly and the days are longer. That makes it warmer: it’s summer. When your part tilts away from the Sun, the sunlight is less direct and the days are shorter. That makes it colder: it’s winter. Spring and fall are in between.

Different parts of Earth have opposite seasons. When it’s summer in the Northern Hemisphere, it’s winter in the Southern Hemisphere.

P.S. Earth’s changing distance from the Sun is not what causes the seasons—the tilt is!
