We have seasons because Earth is tilted as it travels around the Sun.

When our part of Earth tilts toward the Sun, we get more direct sunlight and longer days. That makes it warmer, so it’s summer. When our part tilts away from the Sun, we get less direct sunlight and shorter days. That makes it cooler, so it’s winter. Spring and fall are the seasons in between.

The seasons are opposite in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres: when it’s summer in one, it’s winter in the other. Earth’s tilt—not how close we are to the Sun—is what causes the seasons.

P.S. You can imagine Earth as a tilted spinning top traveling around the Sun!
