
PARAGRAPH 1
THE SENTENCE IS LOGICALLY SOUND BUT MORALLY AMBIGUOUS. IT ASSERTS A CONDITIONAL RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN THE EVALUATION OF LAW AND THE ACTION OF OBEYING IT, YET IT DOES NOT ADDRESS THE CRITERIA FOR DETERMINING WHETHER A LAW IS BAD. WITHOUT A CLEAR DEFINITION OF 'BAD' OR A FRAMEWORK FOR JUDGING COMPLIANCE, THE STATEMENT FUNCTIONS MORE AS A RHETORICAL WARNING THAN A PRACTICAL GUIDELINE.

PARAGRAPH 2
FROM A PHILOSOPHICAL STANDPOINT, THE CLAIM IMPLIES THAT MORAL JUDGMENT OF LEGAL OBLIGATIONS IS AUTOMATIC, IGNORING THE POSSIBILITY OF LEGAL REFORM OR INDIVIDUAL DISCRETION. A MORE NUANCED VERSION WOULD ACKNOWLEDGE THAT FOLLOWING UNJUST LAWS CAN BE A MORAL CHOICE, AND THAT CITIZENS MAY HAVE RESPONSIBILITIES TO ADVOCATE FOR CHANGE RATHER THAN PASSIVE ACCEPTANCE.
