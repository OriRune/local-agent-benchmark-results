Imagine Earth is like a big ball spinning around the Sun. Earth also tilts a little bit, like a top that’s leaning.

Because of that tilt, different parts of Earth get more or less sunlight at different times of the year.

- When your part of Earth leans toward the Sun, you get more sunlight and it feels warm. That’s summer.
- When your part of Earth leans away from the Sun, you get less sunlight and it feels cold. That’s winter.
- In between, Earth is not leaning too much toward or away from the Sun, so it’s spring or fall.

So seasons happen because Earth is tilted as it travels around the Sun.

P.S. The seasons are not caused by Earth being closer to or farther from the Sun.
