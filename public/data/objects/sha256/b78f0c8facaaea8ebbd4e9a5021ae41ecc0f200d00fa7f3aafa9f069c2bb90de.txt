We have seasons because the Earth is tilted as it travels around the Sun.

Imagine the Earth is like a spinning top that leans a little. As the Earth goes around the Sun, different parts of the Earth get more or less sunlight.

- When a part of Earth leans toward the Sun, it gets more sunlight and it is warmer. That is summer.
- When a part of Earth leans away from the Sun, it gets less sunlight and it is cooler. That is winter.
- Spring and fall happen when the sunlight is in between.

So seasons happen because of the Earth’s tilt and its trip around the Sun.

P.S. The Earth is not closer to the Sun in summer and farther away in winter. The seasons happen mostly because of the tilt, not because of distance.
