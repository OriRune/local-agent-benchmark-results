*One chilly Tuesday*, Gerald sneezed so hard that his hat flew off, his eyebrows nearly left his face, and his dignity went to the floor.

He looked around, confused. “Did I just catch something?” he asked.

His friend, who was already wrapped in a blanket and drinking soup, said, “Yep. You caught a cold.”

Gerald gasped. “But I can’t throw it back!”

His friend shrugged. “No, you can’t. That’s the whole problem. You can catch a cold, but you can’t throw it. It just stays there, judging you every time you sneeze.”

Gerald tried to throw it anyway. He made a dramatic throwing motion, but the cold simply stayed in his nose, his throat, and his general sense of misery.

*The cold did not care about his effort.* It just sat there, making him sound like a broken accordion.

By the end of the day, Gerald had learned the most important lesson of his life: some things you can catch, but not throw. And a cold is one of them.
