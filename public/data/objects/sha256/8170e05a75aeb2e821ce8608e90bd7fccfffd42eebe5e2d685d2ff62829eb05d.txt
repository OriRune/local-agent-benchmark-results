Earth has seasons because it is tilted as it travels around the Sun. Imagine Earth leaning a little as it goes around.

When your part of Earth tilts toward the Sun, sunlight shines more directly and the days are longer. That makes it warmer: it’s summer. When your part tilts away, sunlight is less direct and the days are shorter. That makes it colder: it’s winter. Spring and fall are the seasons between them.

The two halves of Earth have opposite seasons. So when it’s summer in the Northern Hemisphere, it’s winter in the Southern Hemisphere.

P.S. Earth is a little closer to the Sun at some times of year, but that isn’t what causes the seasons. The tilt does!
