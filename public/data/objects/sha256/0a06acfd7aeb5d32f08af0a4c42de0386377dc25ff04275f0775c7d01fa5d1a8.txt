If the angle between the two jumps is \(\theta\), the distance from the start is \(4\cos(\theta/2)\), with \(\theta\) uniform on \([0,\pi]\). The distance is less than \(1\) when \(\theta>2\arccos(1/4)\), so the probability is
\[
\frac{\pi-2\arccos(1/4)}{\pi}
=\frac{2\arcsin(1/4)}{\pi}.
\]
The answer is \(\boxed{\textbf{B}}\).
