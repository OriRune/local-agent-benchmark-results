
Imagine the Earth is like a big spinning ball that goes around the Sun. As it travels, it leans a little bit — kind of like a top that’s not perfectly straight. Because of this lean, different parts of the Earth get more or less sunshine at different times of the year.

When the top of the ball points toward the Sun, that part gets extra sunshine and gets warm. That’s summer. When the opposite side points away, it gets less sunshine and gets chilly. That’s winter. The middle part of the ball gets a more even amount of sunshine, and that’s spring and autumn.

So the seasons change because the Earth’s tilt makes some places get more light and some get less, and the Earth’s path around the Sun keeps the light distribution moving around the globe.

P.S. Did you know that the days are actually a little longer in summer and a little shorter in winter?
