PARAGRAPH 1. THE SENTENCE 'IF THE LAW IS BAD, YOU SHOULD NOT FOLLOW IT' IS VAGUE IN ITS DEFINITION OF WHAT COUNTS AS A BAD LAW AND IT FAILS TO PROVIDE CRITERIA FOR JUDGING LEGAL RULES. THE USE OF 'IF' SUGGESTS A CONDITIONAL CLAIM BUT DOES NOT ELUCIDATE THE CONDITIONS THAT MAKE A LAW BAD, LEAVING THE READER UNCLEAR ABOUT THE STANDARD FOR EVALUATION.

PARAGRAPH 2. THE PHRASE 'IF THE LAW IS BAD, YOU SHOULD NOT FOLLOW IT' IMPLIES A MORAL OR LEGAL OBLIGATION TO DISOBEY, WHICH OVERSTATES THE IMPLICATIONS OF A BAD LAW WITHOUT EXPLAINING CONSEQUENCES OR ALTERNATIVES. A MORE PRECISE CRITIQUE WOULD ADDRESS WHETHER DISOBEDIENCE IS JUSTIFIED, WHAT LEGAL PROCEDURES ARE AVAILABLE, AND HOW THE STATE'S INTERSECT WITH INDIVIDUAL RIGHTS, THUS IMPROVING CLARITY AND LOGICAL COHERENCE.
