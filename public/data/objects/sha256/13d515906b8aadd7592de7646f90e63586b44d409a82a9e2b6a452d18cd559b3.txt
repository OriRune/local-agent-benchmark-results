The opening establishes the example \((1,1)\) with \(g=2\), then assumes the gcd condition holds for a fixed pair. In the lemma, the two divisibility arguments yield \(g\mid a-b\); the resulting congruences give \(g\mid 2\gcd(a,b)\), while \(\gcd(a,b)\mid g\). Thus the lemma’s alternatives are \(g=\gcd(a,b)\) or \(g=2\gcd(a,b)\).

For the remaining argument, write \(a=dx\), \(b=dy\), with \(d=\gcd(a,b)\) and \(x,y\) coprime. The lemma bounds the reduced gcd by \(2\). Taking \(K=d^2xy+1\) and \(n\equiv-1\pmod{\varphi(K)}\), Euler’s theorem shows that \(K\) divides both terms in that reduced gcd. Hence \(K\le2\), forcing \(d=x=y=1\).
