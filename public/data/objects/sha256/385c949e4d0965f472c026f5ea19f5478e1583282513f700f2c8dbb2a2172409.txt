PARAGRAPH 1. THE SENTENCE MAKES A STRONG MORAL CLAIM, BUT “BAD” IS VAGUE: A LAW MAY BE UNJUST, INEFFECTIVE, OR MERELY INCONVENIENT, AND THOSE CASES DO NOT ALL JUSTIFY THE SAME RESPONSE. WITHOUT A CLEAR STANDARD FOR JUDGING A LAW, THE ADVICE CAN ENCOURAGE PEOPLE TO TREAT PERSONAL DISAGREEMENT AS SUFFICIENT REASON TO DISOBEY.

PARAGRAPH 2. THE SENTENCE ALSO OVERLOOKS THE RISKS AND RESPONSIBILITIES INVOLVED IN RESISTING UNJUST LAWS. DISOBEDIENCE MAY BE ETHICALLY DEFENSIBLE, ESPECIALLY WHEN IT CHALLENGES SERIOUS INJUSTICE, BUT IT CAN ENDANGER OTHERS OR UNDERMINE IMPORTANT PROTECTIONS. A MORE PERSUASIVE CLAIM WOULD EXPLAIN HOW TO ASSESS A LAW AND CONSIDER PEACEFUL, ACCOUNTABLE WAYS TO OPPOSE IT.
